## Facts for a reviewer

Time-sensitive items (dated as of 2024) are 5 to 8, 10 and 14. No fee, rate, duty schedule or member count is stated as fact; the only count is "over 160 members as of 2024" in the notes.

1. International business: business activities (trade, manufacturing, investment) across national boundaries; international trade (exchange of goods and services between countries) is one part of it.
2. Why it takes place: unequal natural resources; differences in labour productivity and cost of production; specialisation. The Tesoria and Kolmark illustration is a simple output comparison (imagined), not a formal comparative-advantage argument.
3. Scope: merchandise exports and imports; service exports and imports; licensing and franchising; foreign investment. Benefits to nations: foreign exchange; more efficient use of resources; growth and employment; higher standard of living. Benefits to firms: higher profits; better capacity utilisation; growth; a way out of intense domestic competition; improved business vision.
4. Modes of entry, in the order of rising investment, risk and control: exporting and importing (direct, indirect); contract manufacturing; licensing and franchising; joint ventures; wholly owned subsidiaries. Merits and limitations are in own words (exporting: little investment and risk, but transport costs, trade barriers, less control; contract manufacturing: low investment and quick entry, but quality control and leakage of secrets; licensing: little capital, but the licensee may become a competitor; franchising: poor service of one outlet can harm the brand; joint venture: shared investment and risk, but disagreement; wholly owned subsidiary: full control, but heavy investment and the whole risk). Reviewer: confirm against the textbook; the "poor service of one outlet" limitation of franchising is mine.
5. Export procedure, 16 steps in the textbook sequence (as of 2024): enquiry and quotation (proforma invoice); order (indent); creditworthiness and letter of credit; Importer Exporter Code from the Directorate General of Foreign Trade and registration with an export promotion council; pre-shipment finance; production or procurement; pre-shipment inspection; certificate of origin; shipping space; packing and forwarding; insurance; customs clearance through a shipping bill; mate's receipt; freight and bill of lading; invoice; payment through a bank against a bill of exchange. The textbook's excise clearance step appears once in the deck and once in the notes as belonging to the older procedure; it is not taught as current.
6. Import procedure, 10 steps (as of 2024): trade enquiry; Importer Exporter Code; arranging foreign exchange; placing the order (indent); letter of credit; arranging finance; shipment advice; retirement of import documents; arrival of goods; customs clearance through a bill of entry and release.
7. Much of the paperwork is now filed electronically (as of 2024): stated once in the deck (export steps 12 to 16) and once in the notes.
8. Documents and purposes (as of 2024): commercial invoice (seller's bill); packing list (contents of each package); certificate of origin (country where goods were made); shipping bill (exporter's declaration to customs); mate's receipt (ship's receipt when goods are loaded); bill of lading or airway bill (carrier's receipt and evidence of the contract of carriage; a bill of lading is also a document of title); marine insurance policy (cover in transit); bill of exchange (written order to pay a sum); letter of credit (a bank's undertaking to pay against conforming documents); bill of entry (importer's declaration to customs). Reviewer: the pre-shipment inspection step says that "an inspection agency" checks quality.
9. Terms: proforma invoice (quotation, not a demand for payment); indent (detailed order); pre-shipment finance (money lent before shipment); retirement of import documents (paying or accepting the bill and taking the documents from the bank); shipment advice (exporter's notice that goods are on their way).
10. World Trade Organization: established on 1 January 1995; succeeded the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade, in force from 1948; India a founding member (as of 2024); "over 160 members as of 2024" (notes only). Reviewer: please re-check the membership wording and the date facts.
11. WTO objectives (own words): raise standards of living and promote full employment; expand the production of and trade in goods and services; use resources sustainably; help developing countries to share in the growth of trade.
12. WTO functions: administer trade agreements; forum for negotiations; settle disputes; review trade policies; cooperate with the International Monetary Fund and the World Bank. The textbook's technical assistance and training function is not listed (outside the brief).
13. WTO benefits to members (own words, five): predictable rules; settlement of disputes through agreed procedures; lower barriers (choice and lower prices); growth and incomes; fair treatment (rules discourage favouritism). Reviewer: the textbook's list is longer and worded differently; confirm these five are acceptable.
14. Other bodies, one line each (as of 2024): International Monetary Fund (monetary cooperation, exchange stability, short-term help for payment gaps); World Bank, the International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (loans for reconstruction and development); UNCTAD, the United Nations Conference on Trade and Development (helping developing countries to gain from trade and investment).
