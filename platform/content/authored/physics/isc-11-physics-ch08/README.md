## Scope
Follows the ISC 2028 Class XI file `platform/catalog/cisce/isc-11/physics/syllabus.yaml` (chapter 8, topics t01–t05; `removed_topics`: the derivation of E = mc²), not the CBSE chapter.
- **t01** work as the scalar product W = F·S = FS cos θ (θ between the force and the **displacement**), the joule, the erg (1 J = 10⁷ erg), dimensions; positive, zero and negative work; work by a variable force as the area under the F–S graph (W = ∫F dS, strip argument), with a computed handcart graph split into a triangle, a rectangle and a trapezium.
- **t02** kinetic energy K = ½mv² = p²/2m; the work–energy theorem **derived** for a constant net force (F = ma, v² − u² = 2aS), with a one-line statement that it holds for a variable force; stopping distance s = mv²/2F ∝ v² (worked example, computed plot of s against v, numericals).
- **t03** potential energy and the free zero of U (gravitational U = mgh); Hooke's law F = −kx; the work in stretching a spring **derived by the area under the F–x graph and by integration**, U = ½kx² (stretch or compression); conservative forces (path-independent work, zero round a closed path, W = −ΔU; gravity, spring, electrostatic) against non-conservative forces (friction, air drag, viscous drag), with a numerical comparison of gravity and friction on two paths.
- **t04** conservation of mechanical energy when only conservative forces do work (tension and the normal reaction do no work); free fall (v = √(2g·drop)), the simple pendulum (v = √(2gL(1 − cos θ₀))) and a roller-coaster track; the oscillating spring: U + K = ½kA² shown by the work done by the spring and the work–energy theorem (½k(A² − x²) = ½mv²); energy graphs against displacement (U parabola, K inverted parabola, E line, crossing at E/2, x = ±A/√2); x = A cos ωt and the graphs against time (U = E cos² ωt, K = E sin² ωt) appear only as a marked preview of the Oscillations chapter and in no item; energy transformations (table); **E = mc² as a statement only** with c = 3.0 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹ and energy-equivalent calculations (the derivation is not taught, per `removed_topics`).
- **t05** power P = W/t (average) and P = dW/dt = F·v = Fv cos θ (derived), the watt, kW, hp (746 W), the kilowatt hour as a unit of **energy** (3.6 × 10⁶ J); lifts, pumps and vehicles (steady speed: driving force = resistive force; a slope adds mg sin θ).
- Constants stated in the stems: g = 9.8 m s⁻² (every item that uses it) and c = 3.0 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹. No textbook, specimen or past-paper text is used; contexts are Indian or everyday (a porter's luggage trolley, a porter carrying a trunk, a bullock cart, a farmer's plough, a handcart, a cricket ball, a city bus, a mason's bricks, a pump and a tank, a scooter, an electric heater, a locomotive).
- **Left out on purpose:** collisions (elastic and inelastic, momentum conservation; they are ISC chapter 9), the derivation of E = mc² (removed topic), potential energy of a variable gravitational field (gravitation chapter), simple harmonic motion (the oscillations chapter; x = A cos ωt appears only as a marked preview), efficiency, and CBSE-only extras.
