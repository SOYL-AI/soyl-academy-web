## Scope
Follows the ISC 2028 Class XI file `platform/catalog/cisce/isc-11/physics/syllabus.yaml` (chapter 5, topics t01–t05), not the CBSE chapter.
- **t01** force as a push or pull; Newton's first law (law of inertia) and the qualitative meaning of force; inertia and mass; inertia of rest, motion and direction (bus, train, sharp turn, coin on a card); friction and drag as the forces that stop everyday motion.
- **t02** linear momentum p = mv (vector, kg m s⁻¹ = N s); the second law F = dp/dt (average force Δp/Δt; slope of the p–t graph); F = ma as the constant-mass special case; the newton; component equations ΣFx = max, ΣFy = may with a worked crate pulled at an angle; variation of mass near c **as an idea only** (qualitative text and a graph; no calculation asked).
- **t03** impulse J = FΔt = Δp, area under the F–t graph, catching and striking (long stopping time, small force); conservation of linear momentum for an isolated system, derived from the second law for the system; Newton's third law obtained from conservation of momentum for an isolated pair; why the pair does not cancel (different bodies); recoil of a gun V = −mv/M and K_gun/K_bullet = m/M; a bullet embedded in a block.
- **t04** free-body diagrams (weight, normal reaction, tension, applied force); smooth incline (N = mg cos θ, a = g sin θ); a block on a smooth table joined over one pulley to a hanging mass; the Atwood machine (one pulley) with derivations of a and T; blocks in contact (contact force m₂F/(m₁ + m₂), third-law pair); a lift (N = m(g ± a), weightlessness in free fall, cable tension).
- **t05** equilibrium of concurrent forces by the triangle law, the parallelogram law and resolution; Lami's theorem (from the sine rule on the force triangle) with a lamp on two strings; rigid body: ΣF = 0 and Στ = 0, three non-parallel coplanar forces must be concurrent (proved by taking torques about the meeting point of two), three parallel forces possible; the ladder against a smooth wall (N_f = W, F_f = N_w = (W/2) cot θ, with the three lines of action meeting at one point and a loaded-ladder version). The horizontal force at the floor is treated only as a reaction component found from equilibrium.
- Constants stated in the stems: g = 9.8 m s⁻² (every item that uses it). Contexts are Indian or everyday (city bus, ropeway, goal kick, cricket, hockey, tennis, rifle, rocket, lift, traffic signal, painter on a ladder). No textbook, specimen or past-paper text is used.
- **Left out on purpose:** problems with two pulleys (listed in the syllabus file as removed); friction laws, limiting friction, rough incline, angle of repose (ISC ch06); circular motion and banking (ISC ch07); the relativistic formula is shown only as a graph label in an "idea only" slide; CBSE-only extras.
