## Scope
Follows the ISC 2028 Class XI file `platform/catalog/cisce/isc-11/physics/syllabus.yaml` (chapter 6, topics t01–t04; `removed_topics` is empty), not the CBSE chapter.
- **t01** friction as the force opposing relative motion or its tendency; the classical view (interlocking of hills and hollows) and the modern view (real contact only at the tips, adhesion by cold welding, friction as the force to shear the junctions, real area proportional to N, very smooth clean surfaces can stick more); static friction as self-adjusting (f = F while at rest, not μs N); limiting, kinetic and rolling friction; rolling < kinetic < limiting static, with the reason rolling is smallest.
- **t02** the laws of static and kinetic friction (direction, proportional to N, independent of apparent area, depends on the surfaces; kinetic friction nearly independent of speed and area and less than limiting friction); μs = f(max)/N and μk = f_k/N as pure numbers with μk < μs and values that can exceed 1; the friction-force against applied-force graph (computed): a line of slope 1 to the peak, a drop to f_k, a nearly flat line; measuring μ with a spring balance; friction as a non-conservative force (work = −μk N × path length depends on the path, non-zero around a closed path, energy to heat, no potential energy).
- **t03** a block on a rough incline: N = mg cos θ, weight component mg sin θ, friction at rest f = mg sin θ (self-adjusting), sliding down a = g(sin θ − μk cos θ), pushed up P = mg(sin θ + μ cos θ) + ma, held at rest P = mg(sin θ − μs cos θ), pushed down at constant speed P = mg(μ cos θ − sin θ) when tan θ < μ; pulling against pushing a roller at a handle angle φ: N = W − F sin φ and F = μW/(cos φ + μ sin φ) for a pull, N = W + F sin φ and F = μW/(cos φ − μ sin φ) for a push, so pulling is easier.
- **t04** angle of friction λ (angle between N and the resultant of N and limiting friction; tan λ = μs); angle of repose α (tan α = μs, derived on the incline); α = λ shown algebraically and in the figure (the resultant of N and f(max) is vertical and equal to mg); measuring μs with a tilting plank and from a grain heap (tan α = h/r); reducing friction by lubrication, ball and roller bearings and streamlining (and increasing it where grip is wanted).
- Constants stated in the stems: g = 9.8 m s⁻² (every item that uses it). No textbook, specimen or past-paper text is used; contexts are Indian or everyday (an almirah, a wooden chest, a hand-cart, a porter's trunk, a lawn roller, a grain heap, a loaded trolley on a ramp, a skidding car and cycle, a cycle chain, a high-speed train).
- **Left out on purpose:** rolling resistance as a formula (only the qualitative picture is taught), friction in fluids as a law (viscosity is a later chapter), calculus for the minimum pulling force (the plot only shows that the pull dips near λ), and CBSE-only extras.
