## Why it is the standard for Geography

### 1. Deck shape
- **Two parts** (t01–t02 | t03–t04). 29 of 44 slides carry a visual: classification trees (`flow`), comparison tables for every soil and every land-use category, a relief **bar plot** (plains 43%, mountains 30%, plateaus 27%), and one schematic **SVG** of the four conservation methods (terraces, contour ploughing, strip cropping, shelter belt), labelled "not to scale", with colour words in the labels.
- **Worked examples** are the Geography skills the board tests: (1) classifying one resource on all four bases with a trap check (potential vs stock), (2) reading a land-use table step by step (percentages, gross cropped area, comparison with the 33% forest target, a conclusion), (3) **soil map work**: clue → soil → region → shade, label, letter, (4) a 5-mark conservation answer planned against the marks.
- **Region-tagged facts.** Every cause of land degradation is paired with its states and its remedy; every soil with its rock, climate, region and crops — the pairings examiners ask for.

### 3. Sources and data (originality)
All passages are SOYL-written with **imagined people and places**; stems say so. Data are invented but internally consistent (the nine land-use categories add up to the 5,000 km² reporting area; every percentage was recomputed). Real-world facts inside them are accurate: the states named for each cause of degradation, the properties of black soil, shelter belts and thorny bushes stabilising dunes in western Rajasthan.

**Textbook-echo scan.** Geography prose in the textbook is definitional, so the risk is copying definitions sentence by sentence. The author rewrote every sentence whose frame followed the textbook: the definitions of developed resources, reserves and international resources; the "problems of resource use" list; the sustainable development definition (now in Brundtland-style words); the descriptions of gully, sheet and wind erosion, defective farming and strip cropping; the arid-soil and black-soil sentences; the soil-as-a-resource opening; and the Earth Summit sentence. Official category names (net sown area, culturable waste land, current fallow) and the three stage names of resource planning are kept because students must use them.

## Pitfalls this chapter avoids
1. **Stock vs reserves vs potential** — technology lacking (stock), technology available but kept for later (reserves), available and simply unused (potential). Several items are built on exactly this trap.
2. **Fallow time limits** (≤ 1 year, 1–5 years, > 5 years culturable waste).
3. **Black soil is poor in phosphoric content**; **laterite is low in humus** because heat destroys decomposers and rain leaches nutrients.
4. **Contour furrows run across the slope**, not down it (the SVG and an A-R item make this explicit).
5. **Cause–region pairs**: mining (Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha), overgrazing (Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra), over-irrigation (Punjab, Haryana, western Uttar Pradesh).
6. Data answers end with an interpretation, not just a number.

## Verifier pass (2026-10-26)
Every item and part was re-solved from its stem; facts were re-checked against standard Class 10 geography. Findings fixed:
- **Competency labels:** three items had been labelled application but only test a definition or a cause–region pair (pond ownership, hydrogen as stock, over-irrigation in Punjab); they were relabelled understanding/recall, and four genuinely higher-order items were added (resource-category pairs, town growth from land-use change, Punjab's gross vs net sown area, cotton vs rice by soil) plus a d4 analysis (a farmer moving to the Thar).
- **Difficulty:** only one d4 item in the first draft; the black-vs-alluvial comparison and two A-R items whose key is "true but not the explanation" were raised to d4, giving six d4 items and one d5.
- **Narration:** a vague claim that the alluvial plains hold "about the most people per square kilometre anywhere in India" was replaced; a muddled Khadar/Bangar memory trick was rewritten.
- **SVG:** contour furrows were first drawn as arcs that looked like furrows running down the hill; they are now level lines across the slope, and a label that overlapped the hill outline was moved (checked on a resvg raster).
- **Word limits:** four 2-mark model answers trimmed to ≤ 40 words.
- **Originality:** the echo scan above (about 25 sentences rewritten).

## Gaps
- **Maps:** `MapPlace` has no geometry, so the renderer cannot draw the expected shading or a pin for "identify the soil marked A" items; the items are "shade and label" with the region described in `name`. A soil-region overlay for `india-political` (or `india-soils`) would let these be self-checked visually.
- **Plot categories:** the relief bar chart uses x = 1, 2, 3 with the categories named in the axis label and caption; optional category labels on bar series would read better.
- Land-use percentages for India as a whole are not quoted (the data change year to year); the chapter teaches the categories with an imagined district instead.
