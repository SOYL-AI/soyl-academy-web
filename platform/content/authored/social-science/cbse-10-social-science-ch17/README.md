# CBSE Class 10 Social Science, Chapter 17 — Outcomes of Democracy

`cbse-10-social-science-ch17` · release v1 · academic year 2026-27 · family `social-science` (Political Science) · provenance `human`, author `claude-opus-5-5 (subject-producer)` · every item `status: "draft"`

Built to the ch13 Political Science standard. This is an evaluation chapter, so the content is written as a **fair judge**: it states democracy's strengths and its failures (inequality, poverty, slow decisions) with equal clarity, compares democracies with non-democracies on the same outcome, and asks students to reach reasoned verdicts.

## Scope
All three topics: t01 accountable, responsive and legitimate government (42 items), t02 economic growth, inequality and poverty (29), t03 accommodation of diversity, dignity and freedom (37). Items tagged with several topics are counted under each.

## Decks
- **Part 1 (t01–t02), 20 slides, 15 with a visual (8 tables, 5 flows, 2 bar plots).** Fair vs unfair ways of judging democracy, the basic outcome as a tree (accountable, responsive, legitimate), transparency as a flow, "slow but sure" (democracy vs dictatorship on speed, acceptance, effectiveness, correction), the record on accountable government, legitimacy and democracy's own support, a bar plot of **imagined** long-run growth (democracies 4.0, dictatorships 4.3 per cent a year; captioned that real studies also find only a small gap), the other drivers of growth, a bar plot of **imagined** income shares by fifth (4 to 52 per cent), and the poverty puzzle. Worked examples: a 3-mark assessment; averaging growth across imagined countries (both groups 4.15); a 5-mark plan on economic outcomes.
- **Part 2 (t03 + verdict), 16 slides, 11 with a visual (8 tables, 3 flows).** Handling differences peacefully, the two conditions for accommodating diversity (with Sri Lanka as the warning example), dignity and freedom for citizens, women and disadvantaged castes (progress and what remains), subjects vs citizens, why complaints signal success, and an overall scorecard. Worked examples: applying the conditions to an imagined language law; a source on dignity; the 5-mark "better than any other form of government?" evaluation.
- Narration 6–71 words (mean 49), no symbols. Each part has 4 check slides (never used in the tests), a summary and exam tips.

## Evidence and figures
- **No real growth, inequality or survey figures** are quoted. The textbook's tables (growth rates 1950–2000, income shares of named countries, South Asian survey results) are not reused; the deck and items use **imagined, labelled** figures that show the same patterns, and the growth plot's caption says that real long-run studies also find only a small gap.
- All imagined figures were recomputed: (5.1 + 3.2) and (5.4 + 2.9) both average 4.15; 52 ÷ 4 = 13; 45 − 30 = 15 points; 250 ÷ 100 = 2.5 times; 58 ÷ 3 ≈ 19 times (Cerin). Solutions write the arithmetic in words (no Unicode minus or division sign in solution text).
