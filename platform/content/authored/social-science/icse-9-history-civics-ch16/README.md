## Facts for a reviewer to double-check
1. **The Reformation:** a 16th-century movement in Western Christianity that protested against certain practices of the Roman Catholic Church and led to Protestant churches; it began in **1517**.
2. **Practices critics objected to (as commonly taught):** the sale of **indulgences** (certificates said to reduce punishment for sins); the worldly lives of some clergy; the great wealth and taxes of the Church, for example the **tithe** (described as about one tenth of produce or income); the sale of Church offices (**simony**); **Church courts**; services and the Bible in **Latin**, which ordinary people did not understand. All are worded as practices that critics objected to.
3. **New learning:** the Renaissance spirit of enquiry encouraged people to question authority (Renaissance used only as background). **Erasmus**, a Dutch humanist, wrote **The Praise of Folly** (about **1511**); only the title is named and nothing is quoted. **Printing** spread critical ideas and Bibles quickly (no date or inventor is given).
4. **Earlier reformers:** **John Wycliffe** in England (14th century; an English Bible translation) and **Jan Hus** in Bohemia (early 15th century; "also questioned some Church practices" is the only detail). Their fates are not mentioned.
5. **Other causes:** rulers of growing nation-states wanted control over Church lands and appointments; merchants resented Church taxes.
6. **Martin Luther: 1483 to 1546**, a German monk and a professor at **Wittenberg**.
7. **1517:** he made public the **Ninety-five Theses** against the sale of indulgences; **Johann Tetzel** was selling indulgences in Germany (named only).
8. **1521:** **Pope Leo X** excommunicated Luther; at the **Diet of Worms** (1521) Luther refused to take back his views. The Diet is described as an assembly of German princes and officials, and one item stem says the city is on the Rhine.
9. **Frederick of Saxony** protected Luther; he translated the **New Testament into German**, and later the whole Bible. No year is given for the translation.
10. **Luther's main teachings:** salvation by faith (justification by faith) rather than by buying indulgences; the Bible as the main authority; the priesthood of all believers; fewer sacraments (baptism and communion); services (and the Bible) in the language of the people.
11. **Effects:** Lutheran churches spread in northern Germany and Scandinavia; Western Christianity was divided; **Peace of Augsburg (1555)**: each ruler in the German states chose the Lutheran or the Catholic faith for his state (so Lutheranism was recognised beside Catholicism).
12. **Other reformers (one line each):** **Zwingli** in **Zurich**; **Calvin** in **Geneva**; **Henry VIII's Act of Supremacy (1534)** made the king head of the Church of England. The reasons for the English Act are not given.
13. **Counter Reformation (Catholic Reformation):** measures of the Catholic Church to reform itself and check the spread of Protestantism.
14. **Council of Trent (1545 to 1563):** reaffirmed Catholic doctrines; stopped the sale of indulgences for money; required better training of priests in seminaries; insisted on discipline among the clergy.
15. **Society of Jesus (Jesuits):** founded by **Ignatius Loyola**, approved **1540**; worked in education and missions; **Francis Xavier** worked in Asia (linked with ch14; no year or place is given in this chapter).
16. **The Index** of prohibited books; Church courts against heresy (**the Inquisition**), named only and described neutrally (heresy = belief the Church considered contrary to its teaching). No punishments are described.
17. **Results:** the Catholic Church was reformed and remained strong in southern Europe (an atlas item names Spain, Italy and Portugal as mainly Catholic in about 1600, and northern Germany and Scandinavia as Lutheran).
18. **Timeline figure:** drawn to scale from 1510 to 1570; events 1517, 1521, 1534, 1540, 1555, with the Council of Trent as a shaded bar from 1545 to 1563.

## Unsure / gaps
- The Peace of Augsburg line "Lutheranism was recognised beside Catholicism" is a plain reading of the settlement as commonly taught; a reviewer may want it removed if only the rulers' choice is wanted.
- Pope Leo X's excommunication is given as 1521 as briefed; some books date the papal bull to 1520 and the formal excommunication to January 1521. The chapter uses 1521 throughout.
- The Diet of Worms "on the Rhine" detail is extra to the syllabus (kept in one item stem only).
