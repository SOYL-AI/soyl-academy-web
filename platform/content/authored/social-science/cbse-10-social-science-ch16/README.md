## Balance and accuracy
- **Recognition criteria** are taught exactly as the syllabus frames them (state: 6 per cent + 2 assembly seats; national: 6 per cent in Lok Sabha or in four states' assemblies + 4 Lok Sabha seats). The deck and notes add one line saying that the Commission's full rules include alternative routes, so students are not misled.
- **No current list or count of national parties** is stated: the Election Commission revised the list in 2023 and reviews it after elections, and the textbook's list is out of date. Three parties that have long held national recognition are described by founding year and history: Indian National Congress (1885), Bharatiya Janata Party (1980, from the Bharatiya Jana Sangh of 1951), Communist Party of India (Marxist) (1964). Each party's ideology is left to its own constitution and manifesto.
- **No living leader** is named; dynastic succession and defection are taught through imagined cases. Imagined parties have clearly fictional names (Neem, Ujala, Sitara, Sagar; W, X, Y, Z); a first draft used names close to real parties and was changed.
- Coalition history is stated as a pattern ("coalition governments ruled at the centre for long periods after 1989"), without naming alliances.

## Sources (originality)
Every passage is SOYL-written about imagined people and places, labelled in the stem, with `passage_origin: "soyl-original"`. Recognition and seat cases were recomputed (Sagar: 6.5 per cent and 5 seats → national; Sitara: 7 per cent but 1 seat → not recognised; Ujala: 5 per cent → not recognised; Neem: 12 per cent and 9 seats → state party; Corvia's largest party at 28–34 of 100 → coalition).

**Textbook-echo scan.** About 50 phrase occurrences were rewritten: "play a decisive role in making laws", "reduce a vast multitude of opinions into a few basic positions", the without-parties paragraph ("no one will be able to make any promises … no one will be responsible for how the country will be run"), "the rise of political parties is directly linked to the emergence of representative democracies" (an MCQ stem had matched it), "not a democratic option", the two-party and multi-party definitions (two stems had matched the textbook almost word for word), the alliance definition, "allows a variety of interests and opinions to enjoy political representation", "party system is not something any country can choose. It evolves …", "only the official candidates of that party can use that election symbol", the internal-democracy list, "unfair to other members", and the Election Commission order sentence. Names of functions students must use (e.g. "access to government machinery and welfare schemes") are kept.
