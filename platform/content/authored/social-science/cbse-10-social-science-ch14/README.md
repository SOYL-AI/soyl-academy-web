- Narration 9–70 words (mean 52), no symbols. Each part has 4 check slides (objective items that never appear in the tests), a summary and exam tips.

## Sources (originality)
Every passage is SOYL-written about imagined people and places, labelled in the stem, with `passage_origin: "soyl-original"`. Period details are true (Andhra State 1953 and the 1956 Act before the 1957 diary; the 1965 English question and Madras State; a reserved sarpanch seat and the State Election Commission after 1992). Data cases use imagined, labelled figures that were recomputed (5 of 15 seats = one-third; 4 of 12; Amrai's 4/4 meetings and 30% own funds).

**Textbook-echo scan.** About 40 phrases were rewritten after drafting because they followed the textbook's frame too closely even where the fact is standard: the definitions of federalism, unitary government and decentralisation; "existence and authority … constitutionally guaranteed"; "sources of revenue … financial autonomy"; "safeguard and promote unity … accommodate regional diversity"; "pooling sovereignty … retaining identity … increase their security"; "best settled at the local level"; "constitutionally mandatory"; "respect for the autonomy of states"; "made the country more united … administration easier" (kept as a fact, reworded); "together constitute the zilla parishad". Key terms students must use (coming together, holding together, Union of States, residuary, umpire, decentralisation, gram sabha) are kept and defined in SOYL's words.

## Verifier pass (2026-10-27)
Every key and part re-solved from its stem; a key-check script listed each MCQ/A-R key beside its option text and the letter its solution concludes with (two flags were heuristic false positives, re-read by hand).
- **Facts checked:** Union/State/Concurrent examples against the lists (trade and commerce *within the state*; forests, education, marriage, adoption, succession concurrent; residuary to the union); amendment of federal provisions = two-thirds in both Houses + ratification by at least half the states; Article 371 states named as in the syllabus framing (Assam, Nagaland, Arunachal Pradesh, Mizoram); union territories (Chandigarh, Lakshadweep, Andaman and Nicobar Islands); Andhra State 1953, States Reorganisation Act 1956, Maharashtra and Gujarat 1960, Nagaland 1963, Uttarakhand, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh 2000; 22 Scheduled Languages (21 besides Hindi); the 1965 English decision; the 1992 provisions; tiers and heads of local bodies.
- **Accuracy beyond the textbook model:** the textbook says panchayat samiti members are elected by panchayat members; Article 243C (73rd Amendment) fills the seats of panchayats at every level by direct election, so the chapter says only that its members are elected. Zilla parishad membership of MPs and MLAs is described without claiming one national rule. "Hindi is the mother tongue of about 40 per cent" is stated as "less than half" (the census figure has changed between censuses). A worked example's "sales tax by a state" was replaced with land revenue (most sales taxes were subsumed into GST in 2017). The anti-Hindi protests are placed in **Madras State (now Tamil Nadu)**, its name in 1965.
- **Balance:** centre-state relations are taught as a pattern (one-party era, coalition era, a court check on arbitrary dismissal); no party, alliance or living leader is named, and the Supreme Court judgment is described without case politics. Volatile figures (number of elected local representatives, current coalition arithmetic) are not stated.
