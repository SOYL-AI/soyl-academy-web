## Balance and sensitivity
- **Religion:** no religion is named as a party to conflict. Communal events are generic (a forwarded rumour, a speech, a mob); the only historical reference is Partition, as in the syllabus. The newspaper source shows citizens of both communities and the police acting together; evaluation items reward impartial protection of every citizen.
- **Caste:** only constitutional and social categories are used (SC, ST, OBC, Dalit, "historically privileged castes"); no caste is named. Data on inequality are **imagined** and labelled, and items ask students to explain the pattern as an effect of historical exclusion, not to rank communities.
- **Gender:** the reservation debate gives arguments for and the concerns raised (rotation, proxy control, party support) in neutral verbs; no party's position is described.
- **Gandhiji, Ambedkar, Phule, Periyar** appear only as historical figures in the syllabus framing.

## Sources (originality)
Every passage is SOYL-written about imagined people and places, labelled in the stem, with `passage_origin: "soyl-original"`. Period details are true (the suffrage campaign around 1910; Indian women voting on equal terms from the first general election; a reserved woman sarpanch after 1992). Imagined data were recomputed (9.5 − 7.5 = 2 hours; 7 of 9.5 hours ≈ three-quarters; 14 of 200 = 7 per cent; 41 > 34 > 11).

**Textbook-echo scan.** About 90 phrase occurrences were rewritten after drafting: the definitions of patriarchy and feminist (both had matched the textbook almost word for word), "social expectations and stereotypes", "religion is the principal basis of social community", the list in the everyday-beliefs form, "sacred symbols, religious leaders, emotional appeal and plain fear", "ugliest form", "worked to establish a society in which caste inequalities are absent", "caste composition of the electorate", "appeals to caste sentiment", "new consciousness", "incorporating neighbouring castes", "better access to decision making", "divert attention from other pressing issues", "weakening of the position of landlords", "moral values which inform all religions", "improving educational and career opportunities" and "intervene … to ensure equality". Terms students must use (sexual division of labour, communalism, secular state, universal adult franchise, "backward" and "forward") are kept and defined in SOYL's words.
