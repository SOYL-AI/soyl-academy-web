## Sources (all SOYL-original)
An Amritsar schoolteacher's diary (April 1919), a Pratapgarh tenant's letter to his son (1920), a Salt March volunteer's notebook (April 1930), minutes of a Bombay cloth merchants' association (1930 and 1932), a Nagpur teacher's letter after the Poona Pact (October 1932), the description of a Lucknow calendar print of Bharat Mata (around 1930), and a market town's cloth-sales table (1920–22, imagined figures). Each stem says it is illustrative and not a real document; the words are SOYL's and the facts inside are period-true (detention without trial for two years, the Baisakhi crowd, a sanyasi from Fiji, 78 marchers, village headmen resigning near Surat, FICCI and the rupee-rate demand, reserved seats elected by the general electorate, the spinning-wheel flag).

**Balance.** The Poona letter gives both views (separate electorates vs the compromise) with their reasons; the Ambedkar–Gandhiji items stress the shared aim and the differing remedies; items on Muslim participation describe the 1928 negotiations factually (Jinnah's offer, Jayakar's opposition) without blaming a community; the calendar-print item asks students to see why Hindu imagery could make others feel left out.
