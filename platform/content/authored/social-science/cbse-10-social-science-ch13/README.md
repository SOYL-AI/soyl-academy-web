## Scope
All three topics of chapter 13: t01 Belgium and Sri Lanka (47 items), t02 why power-sharing is desirable (15), t03 forms of power-sharing (26). Nothing in this chapter is removed or formative-only.

## Why it is the standard for Political Science

### 1. Deck shape (one part, 25 slides)
- The chapter is short, so it is **one deck**: two stories → why share power → how. 16 of 25 slides carry a visual: a **bar plot** of Belgium's and Brussels' language shares (59/40/1 and 80/20), comparison tables (Belgium vs Sri Lanka, prudential vs moral), a timeline flow from Sri Lanka's independence to the end of the civil war, a **checks-and-balances flow** with labelled edges (who checks whom), and the union → state → local chain.
- **Three worked examples**: comparing two countries from data (point by point, not two paragraphs), a source-based answer that turns events into concepts ("questioning a minister = legislature checks executive"), and the most-asked 5-mark question (forms of power-sharing, one form + one example per mark).
- **Narration** opens with a classroom analogy (a monitor who decides everything) and keeps a neutral voice: "The lesson is not about one community being good or bad. It is about what happens when power is not shared."

### 3. Balance and tone
- Sri Lanka is taught through **policies and their effects** (the 1956 Act, preferences in jobs and universities, the Buddhism clause, denied demands, the Eelam demand, the civil war and its toll on **both** communities), never by judging a community. The exam-tips slide tells students to write the same way.
- No Indian political party, leader or current controversy is named. Indian examples are institutional (courts, union/state/panchayat, reserved constituencies, coalitions in an imagined state).
- Evaluation items ask students to weigh both sides (e.g. power-sharing slows decisions vs. keeps the peace) and reward evidence, not opinion alone.

### 4. Sources (originality)
Every passage is SOYL-written about imagined people and places and says so in the stem. They are designed so (a) and (b) come from the passage and (c) needs the concept. The textbook-echo scan rewrote close paraphrases of the textbook's definitions and sentences: majoritarianism, the prudential/moral wording ("careful calculation of gains and losses", "right to be consulted on how they are to be governed", "acquire a stake in the system", the short-run/long-run tyranny sentence), the Tamil grievances sentence, "distrust turned into widespread conflict", and the judges/executive example. Short standard terms students must use (checks and balances, tyranny of the majority, the very spirit of democracy) are kept.

## Verifier pass (2026-10-26)
Every item and part was re-solved from its stem; figures (59/40/1, 80/20, 74/18 = 13 + 5, 7% Christians), dates (1948, 1956, 1970–1993, 1980s, 2009) and institutions were re-checked. Findings fixed:
- **A broken key (m_farmers):** the draft keyed (d) while the right answer sat at (b), and the solution argued with itself. Options were reordered so the correct answer is (d) and the solution rewritten. All 28 MCQ keys were then re-solved.
- **MCQ key balance:** the draft had 10 b-keys out of 26; four items were reordered (a7 b7 c7 d7 after additions).
- **Accuracy beyond the textbook model:** an item asked which government handles French schooling for a French-speaking family living in Flanders. Real Belgian community powers are tied to language areas and Brussels, so the item was moved to a family in **Brussels**, which is accurate. The definition "elected by a language community wherever its members live" is kept because it is the syllabus formulation (see Gaps).
- **Sri Lanka table:** language and religion shares were listed together and seemed to add to 99%; a caption now explains that Christians are counted within the language groups.
- **Competency:** the draft was at 43% application+; four higher-order items were added (a failing check, a moral vs prudential argument for reserved seats, community government vs state government, evaluating "a 90% majority need not share power").
- **Word limits:** one 5-mark model trimmed to 120 words.

## Gaps
- **Simplification flagged for reviewers:** the syllabus describes Belgian community governments as elected by a language community "no matter where they live"; in practice their powers are tied to language areas and Brussels. The chapter keeps the syllabus formulation in definitions but avoids application items that depend on it.
- **Cartoon items:** the board's Political Science section has a cartoon-based MCQ; cartoons need artwork (licensed or SOYL-drawn), so none are included.
- Map items are practice only and need `europe-political` and `south-asia-political` outline maps in the renderer.
