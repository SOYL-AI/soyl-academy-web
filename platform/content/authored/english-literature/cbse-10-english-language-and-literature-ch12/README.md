## The literature rule: a play taught without its dialogue

CONTEXT §8: summaries, themes, character sketches, literary devices, word meanings and questions — **never the text**; tell the student to read it in the textbook. A play is almost all dialogue and stage directions, so the risk is quoting lines. How this chapter applies the rule:

- **Dialogue-based extracts as commentary on exchanges.** The board's extract questions on this play are usually dialogue. SOYL's 7 `source-based` items are commentary on a specific exchange (the arrival, Lomov alone, the Meadows quarrel, Chubukov joining in, Natalya's hysterics, the dogs, the ending), with every speech **reported in SOYL's words and attributed by name**. Child items ask the dialogue-extract questions (who says what to whom, what it reveals, which device is at work) without any line of the play. All are `passage_origin: "soyl-commentary"`, labelled as such, with a pointer to *First Flight*.
- **No line of dialogue or stage direction** appears in any field; stage directions are described by function ("show his nervousness", "his collapse").
- **Character work** is done through trait/evidence tables, a two-quarrel comparison, an ordering item on Chubukov's changing attitudes and character-sketch questions, all in SOYL's words.
- **Devices with a SOYL-written mini-scene** (a shopkeeper's bow, words and aside), never the play's.
- **Distinctive phrasing avoided:** the cast list's own descriptions, Lomov's self-description of his age and his catalogue of symptoms in the play's order, Natalya's question about his clothes, Chubukov's complaint about fathers of grown-up daughters, the "family bliss" line at the curtain, the history of the Meadows in the play's own genealogy, and the dog insults are not used.
- **Word meanings** are single words or two-word terms, labelled "key words for this play".
- **Textbook pointers:** title slide, "how to study a play" slide, the aside example, summary slide, notes sections 1 and 7, exam tips and every passage stem.
- **NCERT exercise questions are not reused.** Classic questions are reframed (e.g. *what does Lomov's monologue reveal* is asked through what it leaves out; the irony question asks for two examples; *will they be happy* is an open debate).

### Facts to confirm against the textbook
Stated in the content (believed correct; please confirm):
- Chekhov 1860–1904; Russian; trained as a doctor; the play is a one-act farce set in Chubukov's house.
- Names: Stepan Stepanovitch Chubukov; Natalya Stepanovna; Ivan Vassilevitch Lomov; dogs Guess (Lomov's) and Squeezer (the Chubukovs').
- Lomov arrives in formal dress; Chubukov, in an aside, suspects he wants to borrow money; he is delighted by the proposal and fetches Natalya.
- Lomov's monologue: he thinks it is time to marry, wants an orderly life, values Natalya as a housekeeper; he complains of palpitations, sleeplessness and nervous symptoms.
- First quarrel: the Oxen Meadows; Lomov's claim rests on his ancestors having granted the other family's peasants free use of the land; Natalya disputes it; Chubukov sides with her; Lomov threatens court; Chubukov threatens to have the Meadows mowed; insults about relatives; Lomov leaves.
- Chubukov reveals the proposal; Natalya becomes hysterical and demands Lomov's return; she concedes the Meadows when he comes back.
- Second quarrel: Guess versus Squeezer; Lomov says Squeezer is overshot (a jaw fault); Lomov collapses and is thought dead; on reviving he is hurried into the engagement; the quarrel resumes; Chubukov calls for champagne.

Deliberately **left out** because they cannot be confirmed without the text: the characters' ages, the prices paid for the dogs, the money value of the Meadows, which relatives each side insults, Natalya's first words to Lomov, and the year the play was written.
