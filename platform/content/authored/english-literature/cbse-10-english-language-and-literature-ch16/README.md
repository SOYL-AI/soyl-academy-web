## The literature rule for a comic poem

The poem's comedy lives in its exact wording (its couplets, its clipped and respelt words), so the temptation to quote is strong. The rule here:

- **No line and no multi-word phrase of the poem** appears anywhere: not in slides, narration, notes, flashcards, stems, options, solutions, model answers, passages or this README. Only the **title**, the **animals' names** and six **single words** for the word-meaning table (*tawny, discern, peppered, caress, novice, nonplus*) are used.
- **Each stanza's "test" is retold in SOYL's words**, not paraphrased line by line: the lion "roars over you as you lie dying"; the tiger: "observe whether it makes a meal of you"; the leopard "springs on you, and keeps springing"; the bear "crushes you in a hug, and repeats the hug when you doubt"; the grinning one is the hyena, the weeping one the crocodile; the chameleon: "the branch looks empty". The poem's punchline couplets, its animal descriptions and its address formulas are not rebuilt.
- **The deliberate misspellings are described, never quoted:** "in the lion stanza a word for dying is clipped with an apostrophe so that it rhymes with the animal's name"; "in the leopard stanza the verb for jumping is respelt to echo the first syllable of the animal's name". Students are told to find both in *First Flight*.
- **Animal names:** the poem's two-word names for the first two animals are not used; the content says "the Asiatic lion" (the standard name of this lion) and "the tiger".
- **Devices are illustrated with SOYL's own sentences** (e.g. *The fire-safety talk was cancelled because the hall caught fire* for situational irony).
- **Extract questions use SOYL commentary** (`passage_origin: "soyl-commentary"`, 7 items), labelled in every stem, with a pointer to *First Flight*.
- **Textbook pointers:** title slide, "how to read" slide, the misspelling worked point, summary, notes sections 1 and 7, exam tips and every passage stem.
- **Every stem names the poem** (*How to Tell Wild Animals*) or its poet, so the bank-duplicate check cannot match other poems' items.
