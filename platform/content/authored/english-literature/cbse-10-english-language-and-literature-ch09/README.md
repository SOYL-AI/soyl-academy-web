## The literature rule: taught without reproducing the text

CONTEXT §8: summaries, themes, character sketches, literary devices, word meanings and questions — **never the text**; tell the student to read it in the textbook. Maxwell's prose is highly descriptive, and its most memorable effects are its images of the otter (his first-day manner, his joy in the bath, his paw-play, the scene after the box, the Londoners' guesses). How this chapter applies the rule:

- **Summaries in SOYL's words**, organised as six stages (the wish → arrival at Basra → first days → play → the box and the flight → London and a new kind of otter). Facts (Basra, the Tigris marshes, the friend's gift, the second night, the tap, ball and marbles, the airline rule and the Paris route, the rat mistake, the air hostess, walks on a lead, Maxwell's otter) are stated plainly; Maxwell's sentences, images and comparisons are not.
- **Distinctive phrasing avoided.** The first-day manner is described as "kept to himself, showing no warmth and no aggression", not in the text's own paired adjectives; the bath scene as "overjoyed", not the text's phrase; the paw-play as "keeping small objects moving from paw to paw"; the box episode without the text's image of the scene Maxwell found; the otter's scientific status as "a kind of otter never described before", not the text's wording.
- **Extract questions use SOYL commentary.** All 7 `source-based` items have `passage_origin: "soyl-commentary"`, labelled in every stem as SOYL commentary on Maxwell's account, with a pointer to *First Flight*.
- **Devices with SOYL's own lines** (a kitten, a puppy, a parrot), never Maxwell's.
- **Word meanings** are single words, labelled "key words for this chapter" (not claimed as the textbook glossary).
- **Textbook pointers:** title slide, "how to use" slide, summary slide, notes sections 1 and 8, exam tips and every passage stem.
- **NCERT exercise questions are not reused.** Classic questions are reframed (e.g. *why did Maxwell want an otter* is joined to *how he came to own Mij*; the London reactions are asked as *why did they amuse Maxwell*; *should wild animals be pets* is an open debate).

- Two Arab men delivered the otter to Maxwell's room in a sack; it was sent by the friend.
- Mij kept his distance for the first day and slept on the floor; on the second night he slept on the bed beside Maxwell.
- In the bathroom Mij was overjoyed and soon learnt to turn on the tap himself.
- Toys: a rubber ball and marbles; juggling-like paw play; games of his own.
- The British airline would not carry animals, so the route was via Paris; the otter had to travel in a small box at the owner's feet.
- Left in the box before the journey, Mij tore its lining and hurt himself (bled).
- On the aeroplane Mij escaped; one woman took him for a rat; the air hostess helped and let Maxwell keep Mij close for the rest of the flight.
- In London Maxwell walked Mij on a lead; passers-by could not identify him.
- Mij's kind of otter was new to science and named after Maxwell (Maxwell's otter).
