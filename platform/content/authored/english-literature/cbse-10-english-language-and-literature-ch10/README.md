## The literature rule: taught without reproducing the text

CONTEXT §8: summaries, themes, character sketches, literary devices, word meanings and questions — **never the text**; tell the student to read it in the textbook. The story leans on dialogue (Valli and the conductor, Valli and the elderly woman) and on a few vivid descriptions (the scenery, the running cow, the dead cow). How this chapter applies the rule:

- **Summaries in SOYL's words**, organised as seven stages (doorway → research and saving → boarding → outward ride → the town → the dead cow → home with a secret). Facts are stated plainly; the story's sentences, descriptions and lines of dialogue are not.
- **Dialogue is reported, never quoted.** What the conductor, the elderly woman and Valli say is summarised in indirect speech and attributed by name; no line of dialogue appears in any field.
- **Descriptions are not rebuilt.** The scenery is never listed in the story's order; the cow episode avoids the story's "the more … the faster" frame and its image of the cow; Valli's laughter is "helpless laughter", not the story's phrase; her reaction on the way back is "turned away from the window" rather than the story's sentence.
- **Extract questions use SOYL commentary.** All 7 `source-based` items have `passage_origin: "soyl-commentary"`, labelled in every stem as SOYL commentary, with a pointer to *First Flight*.
- **Devices with SOYL's own lines** (a kite, a boat, a wedding street), never the story's.
- **Word meanings** are single words or a two-word term, labelled "key words for this story" (not claimed as the textbook glossary).
- **Textbook pointers:** title slide, "how to use" slide, summary slide, notes sections 1 and 7, exam tips and every passage stem.
- **NCERT exercise questions are not reused.** Classic questions are reframed (e.g. *why did Valli not want to look out of the window* is asked as *why did Valli stop enjoying the view*; the conductor's nickname is asked together with *how she reacted to being teased*; the risk of travelling alone is an open debate).

- She learnt the distance, travel time and fare by listening to neighbours and passengers and asking a few questions; she saved coins and resisted the village fair's treats.

- She refused the conductor's help to climb in; he called her madam with mock respect; an elderly woman passenger fussed over her and Valli disliked it.
- On the way, a young cow ran on the road ahead of the bus and the horn frightened it into running faster; Valli laughed heartily.
- In the town she stayed on the bus, bought a return ticket and politely refused the conductor's offer of a drink.
