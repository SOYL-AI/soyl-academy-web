## The literature rule: taught without reproducing the stories

CONTEXT §8: summaries, themes, character sketches, literary devices, word meanings and questions — **never the text**. How this chapter applies it:

- **Summaries in SOYL's words**, organised by stage (five for Part I, six for Part II). Plot facts (two brothers and a sister; the sister's shorter wings; the herring; no food since the previous evening; the mother stopping short with the fish; the Dakota; Paris; dead compass, instruments and radio; no lights; the runway; only one aeroplane on the radar) are stated plainly; the authors' images and sentence frames are not.
- **No quotation and no dialogue.** The birds' cries, the radio exchanges and the woman's words are never reproduced; the woman's information is reported as a fact attributed to her, as the story does.
- **Extract questions use SOYL commentary.** All 8 `source-based` items have `passage_origin: "soyl-commentary"`, labelled in every stem, with a pointer to *First Flight*.
- **Devices with SOYL's own lines** (*The kettle hissed and spluttered on the stove*; *The lights went out. The lift stopped. Nobody spoke.*).
- **Textbook pointers:** both title slides, the how-to-use slide, both summaries, notes sections 1 and 8, exam tips and every passage stem.
- **NCERT exercise questions are not reused**; classic questions are reframed (e.g. "why did he fly into the storm" is asked as "brave or foolish?"; the mother as "harsh but wise?"; the mystery as "which explanation do you find most convincing?").
