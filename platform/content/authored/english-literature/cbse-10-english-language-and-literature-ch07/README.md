## The literature rule: taught without reproducing the diary

CONTEXT §8: summaries, themes, character sketches, literary devices, word meanings and questions — **never the text**. A diary is a personal, highly quotable voice; how this chapter applies the rule:

- **Summaries in SOYL's words**, organised as six parts (why a diary → no true friend → Kitty → a sketch of her life → school and the promotion bets → Mr Keesing's three essays). Facts (dates, places, names, the Opekta Company, Aachen, the Montessori school, Mrs Kuperus, the three essays, Sanne's help, the poem's plot) are stated plainly; Anne's sentences, images and sentence frames are not.
- **The famous saying about paper and people is described, never quoted** ("an old saying comparing the patience of paper with the patience of people"). Anne's list of what she has, her self-questioning and her essay argument are explained in different words and structure.
- **The three essay titles** are named as titles only: the first two in full (*A Chatterbox*, *An Incorrigible Chatterbox*), the third only described ("a silly title about quacking"), because it is a line of dialogue in the text.
- **Extract questions use SOYL commentary.** All 7 `source-based` items have `passage_origin: "soyl-commentary"`, labelled in every stem, with a pointer to *First Flight*.
- **The diary-entry model slide uses SOYL's own lines.** Extrapolation tasks (Mr Keesing's diary; a diary to an imaginary friend) are fresh writing consistent with the characters.
- **Historical context** (hiding, arrest, death, publication) is given as background, clearly separated from what the extract itself contains; several items test that the extract **predates** the hiding.
- **Textbook pointers:** title slide, "how to use" slide, summary, notes sections 1 and 7, exam tips and every passage stem.
- **NCERT exercise questions are not reused**; classic questions are reframed (e.g. the "chatterbox" essays as a contest between teacher and pupil; loneliness as "is this common among teenagers today?").
