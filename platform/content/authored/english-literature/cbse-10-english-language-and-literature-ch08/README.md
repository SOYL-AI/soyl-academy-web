## The literature rule: taught without reproducing the text

CONTEXT §8: summaries, themes, character sketches, literary devices, word meanings and questions — **never the text**. How this chapter applies it:

- **Summaries in SOYL's words**, organised by stage for each piece. Facts (the *pader*, *kabai*, *bol*, *bolinhas*, twice-daily visits, monthly wall accounts; Kodagu's location, 30 per cent rainforest, September–March, the two origin theories, the Coorg Regiment, Cariappa, firearms without a licence, the Kaveri and mahaseer, Brahmagiri, Nisargadhama, Bylakuppe; eighty crore cups, the two legends, 2700 BC, the sixteenth century, Mariani, Dhekiabari, the second flush) are stated plainly; the writers' images, lists in their order and sentence frames are not.
- **No dialogue is reproduced.** *Tea from Assam* is largely dialogue; what Rajvir, Pranjol and Mr Barua say is reported in SOYL's words and attributed to the named speaker.
- **Extract questions use SOYL commentary.** All 9 `source-based` items have `passage_origin: "soyl-commentary"`, labelled in every stem with the piece's name and a pointer to *First Flight*.
- **Style taught with SOYL's own lines** (nostalgia and humour for Part 1; travel-writing description, facts and exaggeration for Part 2).
- **Textbook pointers:** all three title slides, the chapter-at-a-glance slide, the summaries, notes sections 1 and 8, exam tips and every passage stem.
- **NCERT exercise questions are not reused**; classic questions are reframed (e.g. Coorg's attractions as advice for a group of college students; Rajvir as "a curious and well-prepared traveller: do you agree?"; the tone of the three pieces compared).
