## The literature rule: taught without reproducing the extract

CONTEXT §8: summaries, themes, character sketches, literary devices, word meanings and questions — **never the text**; tell the student to read it in the textbook. This extract is full of famous, quotable sentences (on courage, on hatred and love, on the two obligations, on freedom and the oppressor). How this chapter applies the rule:

- **Summaries in SOYL's words**, organised as six parts (inauguration → flypast, salute and anthems → history and its heroes → courage and human goodness → two obligations → freedom for all). Facts (date, place, order of the oaths, the 140+ countries, which community sang which anthem, the named heroes) are stated plainly; Mandela's sentences, images and sentence frames are not.
- **Famous ideas are explained, never quoted or rebuilt.** The courage idea is taught as "bravery lies in what a person does while afraid", not in the extract's "not X but Y" frame; the hatred idea avoids the extract's list of colour/background/religion; the oppressor idea avoids the prison-and-bars imagery; the obligations idea avoids the extract's parallel lists. "Two obligations" is used rather than the extract's own two-word label.
- **Extract questions use SOYL commentary.** All 7 `source-based` items have `passage_origin: "soyl-commentary"`: an interpretive paragraph about an episode or idea, labelled in every stem as SOYL commentary, with a pointer to *First Flight*.
- **Devices with SOYL's own lines** (*Yesterday the gate kept us out; today it opens to welcome us* for contrast), never the extract's.
- **Word meanings** are single words, labelled "key words for this chapter" (not claimed as the textbook glossary).
- **Textbook pointers:** title slide, "how to use" slide, summary slide, notes sections 1 and 7, exam tips and every passage stem.
- **NCERT exercise questions are not reused.** Classic questions are reframed (e.g. the generals' salute is asked as *why does Mandela draw attention to it*; the anthems as *what was unusual and what did it signify*; the two obligations as a debate, *was he right to put his people first*).
